01 — The buildup

Spending climbed sharply after the invasion of Ukraine.

Combined defence expenditure across 29 European NATO Allies rose from $346 billion in 2021 to $484 billion in 2024 (NATO estimates). The 2025 total excludes Germany, whose figures were unavailable at NATO’s June 2025 cut-off.

02 — The 2% threshold

The Alliance’s long-standing guideline became a floor, then a launchpad.

In 2021 only five European Allies spent at least 2% of GDP on defence. By 2024 that number was seventeen. Every Ally with a reported 2025 figure meets or exceeds the guideline — led by Poland and the Baltic states.

Eastern flank

Poland projects 4.48% of GDP in 2025. Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania all exceed 3% — more than double several Western European peers.

Nordic convergence

Denmark, Finland, Norway, and Sweden cluster between 2.27% and 2.40% in 2024 — a tightly bunched Nordic band above the guideline.

03 — Where the money goes

The buildup is not only larger — it is more capital-intensive.

NATO splits defence budgets four ways: equipment, personnel, infrastructure, and other (chiefly operations and maintenance). Equipment outlays across European Allies grew 63% from 2021 to 2024 — faster than the overall budget.

04 — Germany · Zeitenwende

Three ledgers, one strategic turn.

Germany’s rearmament is visible in NATO statistics, the federal MoD budget chapter (Einzelplan 14), the €100 bn Sondervermögen, and SIPRI’s global ranking. These series are related — not interchangeable.

06 — Methods & sources

Every number is traceable.

Observations carry a source identifier, URL, and access date. Prefer primary publishers. Do not mix NATO, national-budget, and SIPRI figures without noting definition differences.
